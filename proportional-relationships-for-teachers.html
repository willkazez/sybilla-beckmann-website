Proportional Relationships for Teachers

MATH 5035/7035 Spring 2019, University of Georgia, Dr. Beckmann

Meeting times: Tuesdays and Thursdays, 11:00 am–12:15 pm, Room 229 Aderhold Hall.

Course information

Required text: Mathematics for Elementary Teachers with Activities, fifth edition, by Sybilla Beckmann, published by Pearson. Any version—electronic, looseleaf, or hardcover—is fine as long as you have the Class Activities available with you in class.

Course topics: Fraction division; ratio and proportional relationships; statistics, including random samples, measures of center and variation, and comparing distributions; probability, including theoretical and experimental probability and compound events; and, if time permits, number theory, including prime numbers, factors and multiples, divisibility tests, and rational and irrational numbers. The course focuses on aspects of numbers, algebra, statistics, and probability taught in the middle grades and goes deeply into this material.

Course objectives: To strengthen and deepen knowledge and understanding of fraction arithmetic, algebra, statistics and probability, and number theory, including how these are used to solve a wide variety of problems; to strengthen the ability to explain why procedures and problem-solving methods work; to communicate clearly about mathematics orally and in writing; to promote exploration and explanation of mathematical phenomena; and to show that many problems can be solved in a variety of ways.

Class work and writing

Because interactive work in class is an important component of the course, attendance is required. In the event of an illness or emergency, students should contact Dr. Beckmann as soon as possible. Students with four or more unexcused absences may be dropped from the course. Students are expected to turn off cell phones and devote their full attention during class.

This section is part of the Writing Intensive Program. Mathematical writing seeks coherent, logical explanations in which the desired conclusion is deduced from starting assumptions. The graduate teaching assistant, Arvind Suresh, was trained by the Writing Intensive Program to help students develop mathematical explanations and provide feedback throughout the semester.

Assignments and grading

Students should expect to spend at least 6 to 9 hours outside of class each week. Assignments include daily reading, practice exercises, and writing assignments posted on e-Learning Commons. Practice exercises should first be attempted without looking at the solutions and then compared with the book’s solutions. Writing assignments should be typed or neatly written; handwritten work may be scanned and uploaded. Students may work together to develop ideas, but homework must be written independently and in their own words in accordance with UGA’s Academic Honesty Policy.

Work is evaluated for factual correctness, addressing the specific problem, usefulness for teaching, clarity and logic, correct mathematical language and notation, appropriate supporting pictures/diagrams/equations, and coherence. Work is graded on a 10-point scale.

  • In-class and take-home tests and quizzes: 55%
  • Assignments: 15%
  • Final exam: 30%

Letter grades follow the published course scale, ranging from A through F, with the final-exam condition specified for the A and A- ranges.
