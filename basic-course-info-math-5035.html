Basic Course Information, MATH 5035/7035 Spring 2019

MATH 5035/7035 Spring 2019, University of Georgia, Dr. Beckmann

The syllabus is a general plan for the course; changes announced to the class by the instructor may be necessary.

Required text

Mathematics for Elementary Teachers with Activities, fifth edition, by Sybilla Beckmann, Pearson. Electronic, loose-leaf, and hardcover versions are acceptable, provided that you have the Class Activities available during class.

Course topics

The course covers fraction division (Sections 6.4–6.5); ratio and proportional relationships (Chapter 7); statistics, including random samples, measures of center and variation, and comparisons of distributions (Chapter 15); and probability, including theoretical and experimental probability and compound events (Chapter 16). If time permits, number theory (Chapter 8) includes prime numbers, factors and multiples, divisibility tests, and rational and irrational numbers.

The course concentrates on mathematics involving numbers, algebra, statistics, and probability at the middle-grades level, with substantial depth.

Course objectives

  • Deepen understanding of fraction arithmetic, algebra, statistics, probability, and number theory and how these ideas can be used to solve varied problems.
  • Understand and explain why mathematical procedures and problem-solving methods work, rather than merely applying rules.
  • Communicate mathematical ideas clearly, both orally and in writing.
  • Explore mathematical phenomena and explain the reasoning behind observations.
  • Recognize and use multiple approaches when a problem admits more than one solution.

Class work

Because interactive class work is an important part of the course, attendance is required. In an illness or emergency, contact Dr. Beckmann as soon as possible. Four or more unexcused absences may result in being dropped from the course. Cell phones should be turned off and attention devoted to class.

Writing Intensive Program

This section is part of UGA's Writing Intensive Program. Mathematical writing has particular requirements: explanations should be coherent and logical, with conclusions supported by the stated assumptions and reasoning.

Graduate teaching assistant Arvind Suresh was trained by the Writing Intensive Program and served as a writing coach, giving feedback intended to help students improve their mathematical explanations over the semester.

Writing is emphasized because prospective mathematics teachers need deep understanding of the ideas they teach. Writing initial thoughts, revising them, and producing clear and thorough explanations provides another way to develop and refine that understanding.

Assignments

Assignments were posted on the Assignments and Announcements page linked from the main course webpage. Students should expect approximately 6–9 hours of work outside class each week. If illness or an emergency prevents timely submission, students should notify the instructor as soon as possible.

Study groups are encouraged, but each student must prepare and submit their own work and follow UGA's Academic Honesty Policy. Discussing ideas with classmates is encouraged; simply copying another person's work and changing the wording is not. Students should not allow others to copy their work.

Reading

Daily reading assignments are intended to reinforce class ideas and prepare students for the next topic. Retrieval practice is recommended: read a passage, close the book and write what you remember, then reread and repeat.

Practice exercises

Each textbook section includes practice exercises with solutions. Students should attempt them before consulting the solutions, compare their reasoning afterward, and discuss difficult exercises with study partners. These exercises are not handed in, but quiz and test questions may be similar to them.

Writing assignments

Students post regular writing assignments in e-Learning Commons. Work should be typed or neatly written and, when handwritten, scanned before uploading. Assignments may ask students to reflect on class or reading, summarize ideas in their own words, solve problems, or explain solutions. Unless explicitly told otherwise, solutions should be explained.

Mathematical language should be precise. Arvind provides feedback on clarity and precision, and some work may be revised and resubmitted within a few days. Exemplary postings may be shared with the class.

Grading

Written work is evaluated for the following qualities:

  • Mathematical correctness, with only minor and inconsequential errors.
  • Directly answering the question or problem, with focused, detailed, and precise work.
  • Quality sufficient to be useful in teaching a child or another college student, as appropriate.
  • A clear, convincing, logical explanation that does not ask the reader to accept an unsupported leap.
  • Complete sentences and correct mathematical terminology and symbols, with appropriate pictures, diagrams, or equations when useful.
  • Overall coherence.

Observers

Some people attending the class may not submit work or take tests. These are graduate students and postdoctoral fellows observing the course as preparation for eventually teaching courses for prospective elementary or middle-grades teachers.
