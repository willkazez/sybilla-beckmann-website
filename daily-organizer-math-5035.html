Daily Organizer, MATH 5035/7035 Spring 2019

Week 1: Fraction division

Thursday, January 10

Please bring the Class Activities from Section 6.4.

  • Recall the two meanings of division: how-many-units-in-1-group and how-many-groups.
  • Extend these meanings to fraction division.
  • Study fraction division from the how-many-groups perspective.
  • Solve word problems by reasoning about quantities represented in math drawings.
  • Develop a general fraction-division method from the drawings rather than relying only on “keep, change, flip.”

Week 2: Fraction division

Tuesday, January 15

Bring the Section 6.4 Class Activities and Class Activity 5B from Section 5.1.

  • Continue fraction division from the how-many-groups perspective.
  • Use math drawings to reason about quantities.
  • Develop the common-denominator method: give the fractions common denominators and then divide the resulting numerators.
  • Write multiplication equations that compare quantities.

Thursday, January 17

Bring the Section 6.5 Class Activities and Class Activity 5B from Section 5.1.

  • Study fraction division from the how-many-units-in-1-group perspective.
  • Use math drawings and equations to explain why dividing by a fraction can be accomplished by multiplying by its reciprocal.
  • Decide whether a fraction word problem represents division, multiplication, or another type of relationship.

Week 3: Fraction division; ratio and proportional relationships

Tuesday, January 22

Bring the Section 6.5 Class Activities.

  • Continue the how-many-units-in-1-group interpretation.
  • Use math drawings to reason about fraction division and reciprocal multiplication.
  • Distinguish division and multiplication situations in fraction word problems.

Thursday, January 24

Bring the Section 6.5 Class Activities.

  • Continue identifying the mathematical operation represented by a fraction word problem.
  • Investigate whether mixtures can have the same “quality” while differing in size and how all mixtures of the same quality can be described.

Week 4: Ratio and proportional relationships

Tuesday, January 29

Bring the Section 7.2 Class Activities.

  • Solve proportion problems by reasoning about quantities from a multiple-batches perspective.
  • Use double number lines.
  • Reason directly about multiplication and division with quantities.
  • Consider why students should learn approaches other than setting up proportions and cross-multiplying.

Thursday, January 31

Bring the Section 7.2 Class Activities.

  • Continue multiple-batches reasoning with quantities and double number lines.
  • Develop the variable-parts perspective.
  • Use strip diagrams to reason about proportional relationships.
  • GeoGebra sketch

Week 5: Solving proportion problems by reasoning about multiplication and division with quantities

Tuesday, February 5

Bring the Section 7.2 Class Activities.

  • Continue the variable-parts perspective.
  • Reason about multiplication and division with quantities.
  • Use strip diagrams and the GeoGebra sketch.

Thursday, February 7

Bring the Section 7.2 and 7.3 Class Activities.

  • Solve proportion problems using both multiple-batches and variable-parts perspectives.
  • Interpret ratio values as rates in two ways and use those rates to relate quantities in a fixed ratio.

Week 6: Developing equations for proportional relationships and lines

Tuesday, February 12

Bring the Section 7.3 and 7.4 Class Activities.

  • Interpret ratio values as rates in two ways.
  • Use rates to relate quantities that vary together in a fixed proportional relationship.

Thursday, February 14

Bring the Section 7.3 and 7.4 Class Activities.

  • Use the variable-parts perspective to develop equations in two variables for quantities that vary proportionally.
  • Develop familiar equations of lines from the variable-parts perspective.
  • GeoGebra sketches: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Week 7: Developing equations for lines; inversely proportional relationships

Tuesday, February 19

Be prepared to work with GeoGebra sketches on a laptop, iPad, or classroom computer.

  • Continue developing equations for lines from the variable-parts perspective.
  • Use sketches to explain equations for lines through the origin and a given point: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • Explain equations for lines through the origin more generally with this sketch.
  • Explain equations for lines not through the origin with this sketch.

Thursday, February 21

Bring the Section 7.5 Class Activities.

  • Distinguish proportional relationships from other fixed relationships.
  • Study relationships in which quantities vary together through multiplication but are not proportional, including inverse proportionality.

Week 8: Inversely proportional relationships; Statistics

Tuesday, February 26

Bring the Section 7.5 Class Activities.

  • Distinguish proportional, inversely proportional, and other relationships.
  • Compare equations and graphs for proportional and inverse proportional relationships.

Thursday, February 28

Bring the Section 7.5 and 15.1 Class Activities.

  • Continue distinguishing proportional and inverse relationships.
  • Statistical problem solving: formulate a question, collect data, analyze data, and interpret results.
  • Distinguish statistical questions from mathematical questions; statistical questions anticipate variability and are addressed through data.

Week 9: Statistics

Tuesday, March 5

Bring the Section 15.3 Class Activities.

  • Study measures of center: mean, median, and mode.
  • Interpret the mean as leveling the data to a single value.
  • Explain why adding the data and dividing by the number of observations produces the mean.
  • Interpret the mean as a balance point or fulcrum; a tail in a dot plot or histogram pulls the mean toward it.

Thursday, March 7

Bring the Section 15.4 Class Activities.

  • Compare median and mean; the mean is more sensitive to extreme values.
  • Consider why household-income reports commonly use the median.
  • Use measures of center to compare data sets, while recognizing the need to examine variation.
  • Study range and interquartile range, often paired with the median, and mean absolute deviation (MAD), which can be paired with the mean.

Week 10: Statistics and a return to equations

Tuesday, March 19

Work together on the group quiz. GeoGebra sketches:

  • House sketch

Thursday, March 21

Continue working together on the group quiz using the GeoGebra sketches above.

Week 11: Statistics

Tuesday, March 26

Bring Class Activities 15C, 15D, 15F, and 15R.

  • Study random samples and the characteristic shape and mean of their distributions.
  • Compare distributions of larger and smaller random samples.
  • Understand why random samples tend to represent a population and why larger samples are more likely to be representative.
  • Use statistical inference to predict population characteristics from a random sample.

Thursday, March 28

Bring Class Activities 15C and 15D and the class handout.

  • Use means of random-sample distributions to learn about a population.
  • Continue statistical inference and the role of random samples and sample size.

Week 12: Probability

Tuesday, April 2

Bring Class Activity 15D and the Section 16.1 Class Activities.

  • Continue statistical inference from random samples.
  • For equally likely outcomes, interpret an event's probability as the fraction of outcomes in that event.
  • Distinguish theoretical probability from empirical probability; long-run relative frequency approaches the event's probability.
  • Use the NCTM online spinner.

Thursday, April 4

Bring the Section 16.1, 16.2, and 16.3 Class Activities.

  • Continue theoretical versus empirical probability.
  • Count outcomes in multi-stage experiments and distinguish independence from dependence.
  • Study probability of compound events.

Week 13: Probability; Number Theory

Tuesday, April 9

Bring the Section 16.3 and 8.1 Class Activities.

  • Continue counting outcomes in multi-stage experiments and compound-event probability.
  • Study factors and multiples: definitions and methods for finding all factors of a whole number.

Thursday, April 11

Bring the Section 8.1 and 8.4 Class Activities.

  • Continue factors and multiples.
  • Study prime numbers using the Sieve of Eratosthenes, trial division, and factor trees.
  • Study the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

Week 14: Number Theory

Tuesday, April 16

Bring the Section 8.4 and 8.5 Class Activities.

  • Study trial division and when it can stop.
  • Use factor trees and the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
  • Consider why there are infinitely many prime numbers.
  • Find GCF and LCM from their definitions.

Thursday, April 18

Bring the Section 8.5 Class Activities.

  • Find GCF and LCM from definitions and prime factorizations.
  • Use the “slide method.”
  • Solve situations and word problems involving GCF and LCM.
  • If time permits, begin reviewing fraction division from the how-many-units-in-1-group perspective and explain why division by a fraction is equivalent to multiplication by its reciprocal.

Week 15: Review

Tuesday, April 23

Review fraction division.

Thursday, April 25

Continue reviewing fraction division and solving proportion problems in multiple ways by reasoning about multiplication and division with quantities, including applications to probability and statistical inference.
