Assignments and Announcements, MATH 5035/7035 Spring 2019

Please reload this page frequently. Assignments were posted within an hour or so after class.

Week 1: Fraction division

Thursday, January 10: Welcome to MATH 5035/7035.

Week 2: Fraction division

Due Tuesday, January 15: Read the basic course information and consider any requests for modifications; read Section 6.4 and complete Practice Exercises 1–3. Post on eLC the assigned page-259 problem with an explanation using a math drawing, and the assigned Class Activity 6M problem with a math-drawing interpretation.

MATH 7035: Begin brainstorming a course project focused on mathematics related to the course, with a substantial discussion of mathematical ideas and reasoning. Discuss project ideas with Dr. Beckmann.

Thursday, January 17.

Week 3: Fraction division; ratio and proportional relationships

Due Tuesday, January 22: Read Section 6.5 and complete Practice Exercises 1 and 3. Post the assigned problems on page 260 with an explanation of the corresponding math drawings and common-denominator reasoning, and the assigned page-268 problems explaining why multiplying by the reciprocal could be used.

Thursday, January 24.

Week 4: Ratio and proportional relationships

Due Tuesday, January 29: Complete the Section 6.5 Practice Exercise 2; read Section 7.1 and complete Practice Exercises 1 and 2. On eLC, model a simple situation with the equations 5/2 × X = Y and 2/5 × Y = X and use it to explain reciprocal division; also complete problems 10(a–f) on pages 268–269.

Thursday, January 31: Take-home test handed out; due Tuesday, February 5.

Week 5: Solving proportion problems by reasoning about multiplication and division with quantities

Due Tuesday, February 5: Return the take-home test. Read Section 7.2 and complete Practice Exercise 1.

Thursday, February 7: MATH 7035 project proposal due. Submit an outline or description of the proposed project.

Week 6: Solving proportion problems by reasoning about multiplication and division with quantities

Due Tuesday, February 12: Review the four methods for solving the Perfume Problem (two from the multiple-batches perspective and two from the variable-parts perspective) on pages 292–294. Read Section 7.3 through page 302 and complete Practice Exercise 1. On eLC, complete Problems 5c and 6c on page 298, identifying the meaning of one group and, when division is used, whether it is “how many groups” or “how many units in 1 group.” Solve Problem 7 on page 298 in four ways: two variable-parts and two multiple-batches approaches.

Thursday, February 14.

Week 7: Developing equations for proportional relationships and lines; inversely proportional relationships

Due Tuesday, February 19: Read Section 7.3 and Practice Exercise 1; read pages 307–308 of Section 7.4 and complete Practice Exercises 2 and 3. On eLC, solve the sand-and-gravel problems using the ratio values 3/4 and 4/3 from both a multiple-batches/double-number-line perspective and a variable-parts/strip-diagram perspective.

Thursday, February 21: Take-home test handed out.

Week 8: Inversely proportional relationships; statistics

Due Tuesday, February 26: Take-home test due.

Thursday, February 28.

Week 9: Statistics

Due Tuesday, March 5: Read the remainder of Section 7.4 and complete Practice Exercises 2, 3, 5, 6; read Section 7.5 and Practice Exercises 2 and 3; read Section 15.1 and Practice Exercise 1; read the discussion of the three levels of graph reading on page 689. Post the assigned problems from pages 317, 321, and 680, including an explanation using the GeoGebra strips.

Thursday, March 7.

Spring Break: week of March 11.

Week 10: Statistics

Due Tuesday, March 19: Read Section 15.3 and complete Practice Exercises 1–4; read Section 15.4 and complete Practice Exercises 3 and 4. Post Problems 1 on page 700 and 9 on page 717 on eLC.

Thursday, March 21: Group quiz during class.

Week 11: Statistics

Due Tuesday, March 26: Hand in the group quiz; it counts as two quizzes. GeoGebra sketches:

  • House sketch
  • Ramp sketch
  • Line sketch
  • Right triangle in a circle sketch

Thursday, March 28.

Week 12: Probability

Due Tuesday, April 2: Read Section 15.1 and complete Practice Exercises 3 and 4. Post the assigned Class Activity 15F and 15R problems and Problems 5 and 6 on page 680 on eLC.

Thursday, April 4: Group quiz handed out during class; class time provided to work on it.

Week 13: Probability; number theory

Due Tuesday, April 9: MATH 7035 first draft of the course project due. Read Section 16.1 and complete its practice exercises. Hand in the group quiz.

Thursday, April 11.

Week 14: Number theory

Due Tuesday, April 16: Read Section 16.2 and complete Practice Exercises 1, 2, 4, 5; read Section 16.3 and complete Practice Exercises 1–4; read Sections 8.1 and 8.4 and complete Practice Exercises 3 and 4 in Section 8.1. Post the assigned problems from page 742 and demonstrate the Sieve of Eratosthenes through 50, explaining why circled values are prime and crossed-out values are not.

Thursday, April 18.

Week 15: Number theory

Due Tuesday, April 23: Complete the Section 8.4 practice exercises; read Section 8.5 and complete its practice exercises. Post Problem 2 on page 355, Problem 3a on page 356, and Problems 2, 4, 6, and 10 on page 361.

Thursday, April 25: MATH 7035 final project draft due; email it to Dr. Beckmann.

Week 16: Review and Final Exam

Tuesday, April 30: Review.

Wednesday, May 1: Reading day.

Thursday, May 2: FINAL EXAM, 12–3 pm.
