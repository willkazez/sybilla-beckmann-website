Topics, policies, and grading, MATH 2001 Fall 2017

MATH 2001 Fall 2017, University of Georgia, Dr. Beckmann

The course syllabus is a general plan for the course; deviations announced to the class by the instructor may be necessary.

Required text:

Mathematics for Elementary Teachers with Activities, fifth edition, by Sybilla Beckmann, published by Pearson. Any version, electronic, looseleaf, or hardcover, is fine as long as you have the Class Activities available with you in class to work from. The electronic version was available through Pearson MyLab with Course Name BECKMANNFALL2017 and Course ID beckmann41936.

Course topics:

Chapter 10: visualization, angles, circles and spheres, triangles, quadrilaterals, and other polygons; Chapter 11: fundamentals of measurement and converting measurements; Chapter 12: area of rectangles and the connection with multiplication, the moving and additivity principles, areas of triangles, parallelograms, and circles, approximating areas of irregular shapes, perimeter versus area, proving the Pythagorean theorem; Chapter 13: solid shapes, patterns and surface area, volumes of solid shapes; Chapter 14: reflections, translations, rotations, and similarity.

The course focuses on the geometry and measurement taught in PreK through Grade 5; it goes deeply into this material and explores how it connects with mathematics learned in later grades.

Course objectives:

To strengthen and deepen knowledge and understanding of geometry and measurement, how they are used to solve a wide variety of problems, and how they connect with arithmetic and algebra. In particular, to strengthen understanding of and the ability to explain why various procedures and formulas from geometry work; to strengthen the ability to communicate clearly about mathematics, orally and in writing; to promote exploration and explanation of mathematical phenomena; to develop skill with mathematical practices; and to experience that many problems can be solved in a variety of ways.

Class work:

Because our interactive work in class is an important component of this course, class attendance is required. In the event of an illness or emergency, please contact Dr. Beckmann as soon as possible. Students with four or more unexcused absences may be dropped from the course. Please turn off cell phones and devote your full attention during class.

Writing Intensive Program:

This section of MATH 2001 is part of the Writing Intensive Program. The program is designed to help courses teach the writing process within various disciplines. Mathematical writing has special features: in mathematics, we seek coherent, logical explanations in which the desired conclusion is deduced from starting assumptions.

Our graduate teaching assistant has been trained by the Writing Intensive Program to help you learn to write good mathematical explanations. Think of the teaching assistant as a coach, who will give you feedback to help improve your explanations over the semester.

Writing is emphasized because an effective teacher of mathematics needs to understand mathematical ideas well and beyond the level at which they will be discussed with students. Writing initial thoughts and then revising them into clear, thorough explanations helps develop and refine that understanding.

Assignments:

All assignments will be posted on the Assignments and Announcements page. Expect to spend at least 6 to 9 hours outside of class each week. If illness or emergency prevents you from turning in work on time, let the instructor know as soon as possible.

Students are encouraged to form study groups and work on homework with classmates, while adhering to UGA's Academic Honesty Policy. Homework must be written up independently and in your own words; students should participate in developing and refining ideas through discussion and should not allow others to copy their work.

  • Reading: Daily reading assignments prepare students for the next class. Retrieval practice is recommended: read, write down what you remember without notes, reread, and repeat.
  • Preparation for class: Daily assignments may involve Class Activities and posting ideas on e-Learning Commons.
  • Practice Exercises: Practice exercises should first be attempted without looking at solutions and then compared with the book's solutions. They are not handed in.
  • Writing Assignments: Daily writing assignments are posted on e-Learning Commons. Work should be typed or neatly written and should explain solutions unless explicitly instructed otherwise.

During the semester, the teaching team will guide students toward clearer and more precise mathematical explanations. Revisions and resubmissions may be required and must be completed within a few days.

Grading:

Work is evaluated for factual correctness; addressing the specific question or problem; usefulness for teaching; clarity, conviction, and logic; complete sentences and correct mathematical language, symbols, pictures, diagrams and equations where appropriate; and coherence.

Work is graded on a 10-point scale:

  • 10 points: very good; correct, thorough, carefully done.
  • 9 points: work containing only a minor flaw.
  • 8 points: competent; good, solid work that is largely correct.
  • 7 points: work with merit but also some shortcomings.
  • 0 points: significant shortcomings or no attempt.

The course grade is based on tests, quizzes, writing assignments, activity assignments, and a comprehensive final exam. Tests and the final emphasize problems requiring clear, complete, logical explanations.

  • In-class and take-home tests and quizzes: 55%
  • Assignments: 15%
  • Final exam: 30%

Letter grades are expected to be assigned as follows:

  • 9.2–10+: A if the final exam is A- or above; otherwise A-
  • 9.0–9.1: A- if the final exam is B+ or above; otherwise B+
  • 8.8–8.9: B+
  • 8.2–8.7: B
  • 8.0–8.1: B-
  • 7.8–7.9: C+
  • 7.2–7.7: C
  • 7.0–7.1: C-
  • 5.0–6.9: D
  • Below 5.0: F

Observers:

Graduate students and postdoctoral fellows may observe the class in preparation for eventually teaching courses for prospective elementary or middle grades teachers.

As a University of Georgia student, you have agreed to abide by the University's academic honesty policy and Student Honor Code. Questions related to course assignments and the academic honesty policy should be directed to the instructor.
